# Discharge Readiness Checklist

Purpose: Lists what the assigned doctor should review before approving a discharge, and what changes once the patient is discharged.

## Who can approve a discharge

Only the assigned doctor for the admission can approve a discharge. A nurse or a specialist can give information and advice, but cannot approve a discharge. A doctor who is not assigned to the admission also cannot approve it.

## Reviewing open nurse care tasks before discharge

Before approving a discharge, the assigned doctor should check which nurse care tasks are still pending or acknowledged. An open care task can still be completed after discharge to close it out. The doctor should still know which tasks remain open at the moment of discharge.

## Reviewing open consultation requests before discharge

Before approving a discharge, the assigned doctor should check whether any consultation request is still waiting for a specialist response. A consultation request that is still waiting when the admission stops being active is cancelled automatically. The doctor should read any specialist recommendation that has arrived and decide what to do with it.

## Checking clinical record notes before discharge

Before approving a discharge, the assigned doctor should check that the main notes in the clinical record for the admission have been written. Notes that are still unfinished can be added by updating the record after discharge. That later update sets the edited-after-discharge flag and the screen shows an audit banner.

## What changes after a discharge is approved

Once a discharge is approved, the admission is no longer active. New clinical records cannot be created for that admission, and waiting consultation requests are cancelled. Existing clinical records can still be updated, and open nurse care tasks can still be completed.

## AI suggestions about discharge readiness

Any AI output about discharge readiness is advisory only. A doctor, nurse or specialist reviews it and makes every decision. AI does not approve a discharge.
