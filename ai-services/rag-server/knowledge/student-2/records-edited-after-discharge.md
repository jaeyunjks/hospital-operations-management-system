# Records Edited After Discharge

Purpose: Explains how a clinical record can be updated after discharge and what the flag and audit banner mean.

## A clinical record belongs to one admission

Each clinical record belongs to one hospital admission. The record stays linked to that admission and is not shared with any other admission. A clinical record can only be created while the patient is actively in hospital.

## Creating a clinical record after discharge

A new clinical record cannot be created once the patient has been discharged. The admission is no longer active, so the application does not accept new records for it. Only updates to existing clinical records are accepted after discharge.

## Updating a clinical record after discharge

An existing clinical record can still be updated after discharge. A common example is a doctor finishing notes that were not complete when the patient left. The update is saved to the same clinical record.

## The edited-after-discharge flag

When a clinical record is updated after discharge, the application sets a flag on the record to show that it was edited after discharge. The flag stays on the record once it is set, even if the record is edited again. An update made while the patient is still in hospital does not set the flag.

## The audit banner on an edited record

When someone opens a clinical record that was edited after discharge, the screen shows an audit banner. The audit banner tells the reader that the record was changed after the patient left hospital. The banner is there for transparency and does not mean that something has gone wrong.

## AI suggestions about clinical records

Any AI output about a clinical record is advisory only. A doctor, nurse or specialist reviews it and makes every decision. AI does not create or update a clinical record.
